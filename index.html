01

BACKGROUND

Fall of France

In June 1940, France fell to Germany. Britain was now facing Nazi Germany largely alone in Western Europe.

The Strategic Problem

Germany planned a possible invasion of Britain. Before an invasion could take place, the Luftwaffe needed to establish sufficient control of the air.

Operation Sea Lion

Operation Sea Lion was the German plan for the invasion of Britain. Its execution depended heavily upon achieving air superiority over southern England.

Britain's Defence

Britain relied on Fighter Command, radar stations, the Observer Corps, ground control, aircraft production and an integrated command-and-control system.

The German shift toward London changed the pressure placed on Fighter Command and its airfields.

OUTCOME

Britain remained undefeated in the air.

The Luftwaffe failed to achieve the level of air superiority required for Operation Sea Lion to proceed as originally planned.

01 Fighter Command survived
02 German air superiority was not achieved
03 Operation Sea Lion was postponed
04 Britain remained in the war

LESSONS FROM THE BATTLE

01

INTEGRATED AIR DEFENCE

Radar, command and control, communications and fighter aircraft worked as one defensive system.

02

EARLY WARNING

Detecting an approaching threat early increases the time available for decision and response.

03

COMMAND & CONTROL

Effective information flow allowed commanders to allocate limited resources where they were required.

04

LOGISTICS & RESILIENCE

Aircraft, pilots, maintenance, fuel and infrastructure were all critical to sustaining combat capability.

05

HUMAN FACTOR

Pilot experience, fatigue, training and replacement rates directly affected operational effectiveness.

06

RESILIENT INFRASTRUCTURE

A defensive system must continue functioning even when individual installations are damaged.
