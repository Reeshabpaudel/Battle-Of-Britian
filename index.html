01

BACKGROUND

FALL OF FRANCE

France surrendered in June 1940, leaving Britain facing Germany as the principal Western European opponent.

THE STRATEGIC PROBLEM

Germany required control of the air over southern Britain before a cross-Channel invasion could be seriously attempted.

OPERATION SEA LION

Operation Sea Lion was the German plan for an invasion of Britain. Its feasibility depended heavily on air superiority.

BRITISH DEFENCE

Britain relied on Fighter Command, radar, the Observer Corps, ground control, communications and an integrated command system.

THE BATTLE BEGINS

The Battle of Britain began with German attacks against shipping and targets around the English Channel.

04

KEY TURNING POINTS

01

RADAR & EARLY WARNING

Early detection gave Fighter Command additional time to identify and respond to approaching formations.

02

ATTACKS ON AIRFIELDS

German attacks placed pressure on RAF airfields, aircraft and operational infrastructure.

03

SHIFT TO LONDON

From 7 September, major German attacks shifted toward London and other urban targets.

04

15 SEPTEMBER

Major daylight attacks made 15 September an important date in the campaign.

05

OUTCOME

Air superiority was not achieved.

The Luftwaffe did not obtain the level of air superiority required to allow Operation Sea Lion to proceed as planned.

01 Fighter Command survived
02 German air superiority was not achieved

06

LESSONS FROM THE BATTLE

01

INTEGRATED AIR DEFENCE

Radar, command and control, communications and fighter aircraft operated as an interconnected system.

02

EARLY WARNING

Early detection increased the time available for decision-making and fighter interception.

03

COMMAND & CONTROL

Information was filtered and passed through the command system so that available fighters could be directed.

04

LOGISTICS

Aircraft production, maintenance, fuel, spare parts and trained personnel were essential to sustaining operations.

05

HUMAN FACTOR

Pilot training, experience, fatigue and replacement rates affected operational capability.

06

RESILIENCE

Defensive systems needed to continue operating despite damage to individual installations.
